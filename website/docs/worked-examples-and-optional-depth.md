# Worked Examples and Optional Depth

Use these cards after the corresponding core teaching. They supply the scenario and a sample response; no external research, new account, real-world contact, or private disclosure is required. The examples are fictional teaching cases, not real measurements, medical guidance, legal rulings, financial advice, or promises of outcomes.

## Two kinds of optional work

- **Supplied practice:** the scenario, sample response, and depth question are on this page. Allow about 15–20 minutes for one card, with paper or oral explanation. Read the card before the session; a calculator is optional for arithmetic.
- **Open research prompt:** a request to locate or construct missing teaching material, such as outside sources, interview records, or a real-case packet. This is not supplied instruction. A tool activity with complete steps is still supplied instruction, even when an adult must prepare the device or account. An adult must select and verify materials, arrange access, and plan additional time first. Use the supplied card instead when those materials are unavailable.

Choose depth by readiness and interest, not age alone. Learners aged 10–12 can use the core teaching without taking every technical add-on; older-learner work remains optional. The depth questions are not prerequisites for the next core week or criteria for its checkpoint.

## How to use a card

1. Read the scenario and ask for a reasoned answer.
2. Let the learner draw, speak, sort, or write using the fictional case.
3. Compare with the sample: notice the evidence and the limits, not matching wording.
4. If ready, change the stated assumption using the depth question.
5. If the core idea is unclear, return to the linked lesson before adding complexity. A private or paper artifact is enough.

Sample responses illustrate reasoning, not scripts to memorize or the only defensible answer to an open choice. They do not require a learner to experience or disclose the situation.

## Week 1
**Core lesson:** [Week 1: Sunlight Detective (How Solar Energy Drives Everything)](./week01-the-planetary-engine.md)

**Supplied scenario and question:** Trace energy through Sun → leaf → rabbit. What is moving or changing?

**Illustrative response:** Sunlight energy supports plant growth and then can be transferred through food. Matter and energy have different paths; energy is not endlessly recycled in a closed loop.

**Optional depth:** Draw a separate matter arrow and an energy arrow and state where the simplified drawing leaves detail out.

**Facilitator check:** Ask which stated fact or assumption supports the explanation. If that link is missing, model it with the sample and invite a second explanation. Assess reasoning and limits, not speed, exact wording, or personal experience.

## Week 2
**Core lesson:** [Week 2: Energy Changes Form (Energy Cannot Be Created or Destroyed)](./week02-thermodynamics-basics.md)

**Supplied scenario and question:** An invented device receives 100 energy units, delivers 30 as useful motion, and transfers 70 as heat. Does energy disappear?

**Illustrative response:** No: 30 + 70 = 100. Heat is still energy, although it may be less useful for the intended task. The figures are a model, not a device specification.

**Optional depth:** Change useful motion to 40 with the same input and account for the remaining 60.

**Facilitator check:** Ask which stated fact or assumption supports the explanation. If that link is missing, model it with the sample and invite a second explanation. Assess reasoning and limits, not speed, exact wording, or personal experience.

## Week 3
**Core lesson:** [Week 3: Build a Tiny World in a Jar (A Closed-Loop Micro-Ecosystem)](./week03-the-terrarium-test.md)

## Week 5
**Core lesson:** [Week 5: Carbon the Shape-Shifter (How Carbon Moves Through Living Systems)](./week05-the-carbon-cycle.md)

**Supplied scenario and question:** Connect fossil-fuel burning with atmospheric CO₂ and warming using two different arrow colors.

**Illustrative response:** Burning transfers stored carbon into air. Added CO₂ affects infrared energy escape and Earth’s energy balance; carbon matter does not create energy from nothing.

**Optional depth:** Explain why one cold local day cannot decide a global long-term trend, and why CO₂ is not a solid lid.

**Facilitator check:** Ask which stated fact or assumption supports the explanation. If that link is missing, model it with the sample and invite a second explanation. Assess reasoning and limits, not speed, exact wording, or personal experience.

## Week 6
**Core lesson:** [Week 6: The Locked Food Plants Can't Open (The Nitrogen Cycle)](./week06-the-nitrogen-cycle.md)

**Supplied scenario and question:** A plant is surrounded by air containing nitrogen, but cannot simply use most nitrogen gas directly. What conversion matters?

**Illustrative response:** Fixation changes nitrogen gas into usable compounds; other organisms and processes move nitrogen through soil and living things. Saying there is much nitrogen in air does not mean it is immediately usable.

**Optional depth:** Trace one plant–animal–soil route and distinguish conversion from creating new nitrogen matter.

**Facilitator check:** Ask which stated fact or assumption supports the explanation. If that link is missing, model it with the sample and invite a second explanation. Assess reasoning and limits, not speed, exact wording, or personal experience.

**Supplied scenario and question:** In the supplied habitat model, removing leaf litter removes beetles’ habitat, frogs’ required cover, and birds’ food. What survives all listed needs?

## Week 13

## Week 18
**Core lesson:** [Week 18: Share Your Plan With Someone Real](./week18-the-community-pitch.md)

**Supplied scenario and question:** Share the fictional scrap-paper proposal with a drawn audience card instead of contacting a real person. What is a complete explanation?

**Illustrative response:** State the problem, return path, roles, measured trial, uncertainty, and next step. Distinguish actual results from targets and model values.

**Optional depth:** Include one affected user’s need; a real public pitch is optional, and a paper/private presentation can show the learning.
