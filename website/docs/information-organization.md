# Information Organization Skills for Big Systems

Environmental systems literacy uses loops, maps, observations, data, causes, effects, audits, proposals, and models. Information Organization helps kids make big systems visible — without making one child responsible for fixing everything.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Information Organization Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/information-organization), connected to the systems thinking this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Big systems become easier when information is grouped.** One pile per part of the system.
- **Loops, maps, and diagrams show relationships.** Arrows say what sentences hide.
- **Audit data needs categories.** Counting is easier with piles to count into.
- **Proposals need evidence and source tracking.** Claims stay connected to where they came from.
- **No child has to organize or solve a whole system alone.**

## When this shows up

- When mapping a system loop
- When sorting audit observations
- When tracking causes and effects
- When organizing proposal evidence
- When comparing solutions
- When revising a model after feedback

## Tools that help

- **Loop diagram** — one loop at a time.
- **Cause/effect map** — arrows between what happens and why.
- **Audit category table** — piles for observations.
- **Evidence/source tracker** — claims kept next to their sources.
- **Proposal organizer** — evidence, questions, and next steps with homes.
- **One-system-part focus** — organize a piece, not the planet.

:::tip Information Organization Moment
A big system becomes easier when you organize one part at a time. Try sorting observations into causes, effects, questions, and evidence. The system is still big, but your thinking has a map.
:::

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not a replacement for support
These are everyday information organization tools, not therapy, disability evaluation, academic intervention, legal advice, medical advice, or a replacement for trusted adults, educators, caregivers, accommodations, or qualified support. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when information feels messy or hard to organize — and no child has to organize or fix a whole system by themselves. If information feels consistently overwhelming, inaccessible, unsafe, private, sensitive, or too big, involve a trusted adult and appropriate support.
:::
